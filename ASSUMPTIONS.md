# Assumptions

Every number in this project that is not derived from the data is listed here, with
whether it is verified, assumed, or arbitrary-but-declared. Started in Phase 1,
finished in Phase 13; the write-up quotes this file rather than restating it.

The reason it exists this early: "state your assumptions, don't bluff them" is a
submission requirement, and the moment to write down a rate is when you pick it,
not when a judge asks. `tools/acceptance.py` gate 7 fails the build if a required
topic goes missing from this file.

**Status key** — 🔴 unverified assumption, needs checking before the final run ·
🟡 arbitrary but declared and frozen · 🟢 verified or structurally guaranteed

---

## Money and arithmetic

| # | Assumption | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **All money is integer paise.** No floats anywhere, at any point, including intermediate values. Enforced at the boundary by `money.paise()`, which rejects `float` and `bool`, and re-checked after the CSV round-trip by invariant I5. | 🟢 |
| 2 | **Rates are integer basis points**, never percentages as floats. 2% is `200`, 18% is `1800`. | 🟢 |
| 3 | **Rounding is half-up at the paisa**, computed as `(amount × bps + 5000) // 10000`. Exact integer arithmetic — no `Decimal` context to misconfigure. Half-up rather than banker's rounding because it is what invoice arithmetic conventionally uses and because it is the rule a human checking the sum by hand would apply. **The choice is observable, and #4a says how often:** the two rules differ only on an exact half, which arises **81.5 times per 1,000-payment run** — so this is a live convention, not a distinction without a difference. It stays 🟡 because the convention is declared rather than verified against a bank's actual behaviour; what #4a verifies is that both sides of this system apply *the same* rule, which is the part that could have produced a false exception. | 🟡 |
| 4 | Rounding is defined for **non-negative operands only**. Half-up on a negative is ambiguous, every rate in this system applies to a positive amount, and `mul_bps` asserts rather than guessing. | 🟢 |
| 4a | **`--rounding-edge` is declined, and the reason is structural rather than budgetary: the flag's premise does not exist.** It was specified to make the two sides disagree about rounding. They cannot. `mul_bps` is defined **once**, in `hisaab/common/money.py`, and imported by both the generator (`story.py`) and the matcher (`fees.py`, `tier1.py`); only the *rates* are duplicated, which is the disagreement #5–#9 already cover. So no flag can drive a wedge between two callers of one function, and a flag that pretended to would be measuring its own mock. **Nor would it be switching on a dormant behaviour.** Counted by wrapping `mul_bps` over seeds 1/2/3/42 × n=1000 with `--fees --settlement-delay --tds` (12,000 calls), rounding already fires constantly — and *how* constantly depends entirely on which condition you mean, which is why all three are stated: the division is inexact **2,183×/run** (2,170–2,206), half-up beats plain truncation **1,125×/run** (1,087–1,146), and an exact half — the only case where half-up and banker's rounding actually disagree — arises **81.5×/run** (76–87). A number here without its condition is a number that means whatever the reader assumes. **The consequence is a check that stays honest.** `rounding_edge` remains permanently declared-and-inert, so `MessFlags.unimplemented() == {"rounding_edge"}` keeps a real subject and `config.py`'s refusal loop keeps a real iteration — had the dial completed at 13 of 13, both would have gone quietly vacuous. Measured 2026-08-29. | 🟢 declined |

The worked example from the track spec lands correctly under this rule: ₹1,111 at
200 bps is a ₹22.22 fee, and 1800 bps GST on that fee is 399.96 paise → 400.

## Fee model — the numbers most likely to be wrong

Declared in `config.FeeConfig`, re-declared independently in `matcher/fees.py`, and
**not used in Phase 1** (clean mode has zero fees, not "fees applied consistently").

**Checked against <https://razorpay.com/pricing/> on 2026-08-26, and two of the five
were wrong.** The whole point of the status column is to make this moment cheap, so
the corrections are recorded in place rather than quietly overwritten.

| # | Assumption | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Card and wallet: **200 bps** (2%) | 🟢 verified |
| 6 | Netbanking: **200 bps** (2%) — **corrected from 190.** The guess was that netbanking is priced below cards; the page prices every standard domestic instrument at one flat 2%. | 🟢 verified |
| 7 | UPI: **200 bps** (2%) — **corrected from 0.** UPI carries **zero MDR** by mandate, which is true and widely repeated, but Razorpay's own **2% platform fee still applies** on the standard payment-gateway rail. Zero MDR and zero fee are different claims and the original entry conflated them. | 🟢 verified |
| 7a | POS UPI and RuPay debit: **0 bps** (0.00%) — the zero-rated rail, on a POS terminal rather than the PG rail. | 🟢 verified |
| 7b | Corporate / business credit cards: **215 bps** (2.15%) | 🟢 verified |
| 7c | International cards: **300 bps** (up to 3%), by card **origin**, still settled in INR. Currency conversion is a separate concern — `--fx` is **Phase 8** (#22g), and it moves the *rate between capture and settlement*, not this rate. Said "Phase 6" until 2026-08-29; the flag was never in Phase 6 and the two numbers are independent — a 300 bps origin fee is charged on an international card whether or not the rate moved. | 🟢 verified |
| 8 | GST: **1800 bps** (18%), charged **on the fee**, not on the gross | 🟢 verified |
| 9 | TDS: **10 bps** (0.1%) under §194-O, only where `--tds` is on — **corrected from 100.** The section ran at 1% from its introduction on 2020-10-01, and the **Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 cut it to 0.1% effective 2024-10-01**; the old value was the pre-amendment position, carried since Phase 1 because nothing read it until Phase 6. Verified 2026-08-27. | 🟢 verified |
| 9a | **That a payment aggregator withholds §194-O on a merchant settlement at all is a separate claim from the rate, and it is *not* verified.** §194-O binds the *e-commerce operator* whose platform facilitates the sale, which is the merchant's storefront rather than the payment rail underneath it, and CBDT has issued guidance specifically on payment gateways that this project has not read. So the doubt is recorded **in the direction it actually points**: a pure payment aggregator most likely does **not** withhold this, and `--tds` should be read as modelling *a* rate-derived withholding wedge rather than as a claim about Razorpay's tax position. What the flag tests does not depend on the statute being the right one — it is a third gross/net wedge whose magnitude is **derivable from a declared rate**, which is what exercises multi-rule decomposition (#25) and the abstention when two rules both close a gap. §194-O supplies the citation for the *number*; the number is what the matcher re-derives. | 🟡 declared |

**Which of these is a "verified fact" and which is still an assumption.** The rates
are sourced and dated, not permanently true: these are **list prices**, real rates are
negotiated at volume, and Indian MDR structures move with NPCI/RBI policy. That is why
every rate is overridable from the matcher's command line (`--fee-bps METHOD=BPS`)
rather than compiled in. #9 was the last 🔴 in this block and Phase 6 closed it, but the
correction is worth reading twice, because it is the one place where **the same publisher was
right on one page and stale on another**: Razorpay's pricing page carries the fee rates and is
current, while Razorpay's own §194-O explainer still states 1% and does not mention the 2024
amendment. Citing a source is therefore not enough — the citation has to be **dated**, which
is why every row here carries a verification date rather than only a URL. And #9a keeps the
part that a rate alone cannot settle: the number is verified, the claim that this deduction
belongs on a payment aggregator's settlement is not.

**What the corrected TDS rate does and does not shrink.** At 10 bps TDS is now the
**smallest** of the deductions — against 200 bps for the fee and 36 bps *effective* for
GST-on-fee (18% of 200) — where at 100 bps it outweighed GST. It does **not** become
invisible, and the reason is worth stating because the Phase 6 plan got it wrong by a factor
of 100: `AMOUNT_BANDS` is denominated in **rupees**, not paise (`config.py:56`), so the
smallest gross this generator can draw is ₹100 = 10,000 paise and its TDS is **10 paise**.
Measured on the committed run: **0 of 60 settlements** carry a zero TDS term, min 12p, max
9,506p. The plan had predicted `mul_bps(100, 10) == 0` across the 45%-weighted modal band and
called the term "mistakable for rounding"; that arithmetic read the band edges as paise. The
consequence is a **stronger** assertion available to the gate, not a weaker one — no
settlement carries a zero term, so the flag cannot be a no-op on any row.

**Per-member rounding stays a real discipline at 10 bps, and stays the same size as at 200.**
Measured over 20,000 synthetic batches of 2–4 members drawn from the modal band: the
per-member sum and the batch-total rate differ on **32.7%** of batches, by at most **2
paise** — the same character as the fee's own divergence, not a rounding artefact that
swallows the whole term. (The plan expected 0 paise per member against 1–2 on the total,
which followed from the same 100× slip; a fixture where per-member rounds to zero would need
a gross under 50 paise and is unreachable above the ₹100 floor.)

**Why #7 was the expensive one, and what it was costing the evidence.** A wrong rate
that makes a fee *smaller* is not a bookkeeping slip; it removes rows from the test.
A zero-rated payment settles at its gross, so its residual is zero even under `--fees`
and it resolves with no fee model at all. UPI at 0 bps was **36% of rows** on a `--fees`
run — over a third of the sample was proving nothing about the fee model while counting
toward a coverage figure that looked like it did. With the free rail moved to `pos_upi`
that share is **~6%**, which makes `--fees` a materially sharper test than the number
alone suggests. This is also why the claim "the fee model moved the coverage number" has
to be measured **per method**: in aggregate, the zero-rated rows silently carry it.

The zero-rated branch was kept rather than deleted — `story.py` and `matcher/fees.py`
both take it, and a rule named `no deduction` is what distinguishes *nothing was
withheld* from *nothing was checked*. It is now exercised by something the page
actually confirms. Note that neither module names a method: the branch is reached by
**rate**, which is the only reason correcting a rate was a small change here.

## Dates and the calendar

| # | Assumption | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Business days are Monday–Friday.** The holiday set is **empty**. | 🟡 |
| 11 | Payment capture happens on a business day, between **09:00 and 21:00 IST**. | 🟡 |
| 12 | **IST is a fixed UTC+05:30 offset.** Implemented with `timezone(timedelta(hours=5, minutes=30))`, deliberately not `zoneinfo.ZoneInfo("Asia/Kolkata")` — bare Windows Python ships no tzdata. IST has never observed DST, so the fixed offset is exact, not an approximation. | 🟢 |
| 13 | `settled_on` and `value_date` are **dates**; `captured_at` is an **ISO-8601 UTC timestamp** with a trailing `Z`. This mirrors how real exports differ from each other. | 🟢 |
| 14 | The month generated comes from `--month`, never from `date.today()`. | 🟢 |
| 15 | **T+0 in clean mode.** `--settlement-delay` introduces T+n, implemented in Phase 4. | 🟢 |
| 15a | **`--settlement-delay` turns on two different delays, and conflating them is the mistake to avoid.** `settlement_delay_days` (**T+2**) is capture → `settled_on`, the gateway's own settlement cycle. `posting_lag_days` (**1 business day**) is `settled_on` → the bank credit's `value_date`, the statement posting lag. Both are separately overridable and both default to 0 with the flag off. | 🟢 |
| 15b | **Only the posting lag is visible to the matcher's date window.** The bank credit is derived from the settlement, so a matcher joining credits to settlements sees `posting_lag_days` and never `settlement_delay_days` — which is why `--window 0` fails under a non-zero posting lag while T+2 alone costs nothing. Recorded because "T+2" is the number a reader expects the window to need, and it is the wrong one. | 🟢 |
| 16 | T+n rolls forward off non-business days: money captured on a Saturday settles on the following business day, never on the Saturday. Applied to **both** delays in 15a, so a Friday capture at T+2 with a 1-day lag lands the following Wednesday, not Sunday/Monday. | 🟡 |

On #10: a real Indian bank calendar has gazetted holidays that vary by state, and
inventing a list would be bluffing. `BusinessCalendar` takes an injectable holiday
set, so a real one drops in without a code change if it becomes worth having.

On #11: the clamp is load-bearing, not cosmetic. `captured_at` is UTC and
`settled_on` is a date, so a 00:30 IST capture serialises to the *previous* UTC day
— and Phase 4 would then see a one-day gap and blame its own delay model for a
discrepancy the timezone created. Within 09:00–21:00 IST the UTC date and the IST
date are always equal. Phase 4 may relax this, but deliberately.

## Identifiers and file shapes

| # | Assumption | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | **IDs are fixed-width 4**: `pay_0001`, `ord_0001`, `setl_0001`, `rfnd_0001`, `C0001`. Appendix A of the spec mixes `setl_01` and `C001` widths; those stop sorting lexicographically past 99 records, so one width is picked and never revisited. | 🟡 |
| 18 | **The bank statement has exactly four columns**: `row_id`, `value_date`, `amount_paise`, `narration`. No gateway identifier, no record count, no gross amount. Header pinned by invariant I9. | 🟢 |
| 19 | Bank narration comes in **four styles**, varying by channel and format. Real statements are inconsistent across banks; a single format would make the Phase 3 parser one regex and leave Phase 10's narration-parsing job with nothing to do. Style variance changes no amount and no date, so clean mode stays clean where it counts. | 🟡 |
| 20 | **UTR is present on every settlement row in Phase 1** and appears as a 4-digit tail in the narration. It is the one legitimate link between the gateway and the bank, and it is realistic — a UTR really does appear in both places. **Phase 8's `--utr-patchy` now removes that tail from 15% of *bank narrations* — and only removes it** (#22i): all twelve (style, channel) maskings render with no digit at all, so nothing is left for a parser to half-read. This row previously said "missing or truncated"; the truncated half was never true of what the generator emits, and `matcher/normalize.py` still parses a truncated tail because a real statement can carry one, not because this flag produces one. `settlements.csv` keeps its `utr` column intact throughout — stripping it would make the answer key lie about its own data. **Present is not the same as unique per settlement, from Phase 4b on:** `--dup-amounts` makes each planted pair *share* one UTR on purpose, and that sharing is the load-bearing half of the flag — see #24e. So the tail is unique everywhere except across a planted pair, where it is deliberately not. | 🟡 |
| 21 | `settlement_items.csv` is a **fifth file**, declaring which payments each settlement contains. Appendix A gives `settlements.csv` no membership column, but Tier 2 needs the settlement report to declare its contents, and real gateway reports do have a line-item export. It links payments↔settlements only; the settlements↔bank link stays the hard part. This is what `--settlement-report-late` withholds. **Built in Phase 5, not Phase 8 as first planned**, because Tier 2 has nothing to search for while every settlement declares its own contents. What the flag withholds is the *declaration a judge's files carry*, never the membership itself: truth still publishes it and every in-memory invariant still runs unchanged, which is what lets the scorer grade a searched answer against the real one. | 🟡 |
| 22 | `refunds.csv` is written **header-only** in clean mode. An empty file that exists is better than a missing one — the loader gets exercised from Phase 3 rather than blowing up in Phase 6. | 🟢 |
| 22a | **Netted refunds (`--netted-refunds`, Phase 6): 10% of payments carry one, drawn at 20–60% of that payment's gross.** A share rather than a rate on every row, because a flag that refunded everything would make "the refund term" and "every row" the same set, and nothing downstream could then tell a refund-aware matcher from one that had absorbed the term into its fee model. The magnitude is **converted to paise at draw time and frozen**, never re-derived from the gross later: `_make_nets_unique` nudges a gross by a paisa to keep two settlements off one net, and a refund stored as a rate would move with every nudge — the net would chase the value the nudge is trying to place, a loop with no guaranteed fixed point. The cost is that a refund is not exactly *x*% of the final gross on a nudged row, which is invisible in the data. **Exactly one refund per run is an orphan** — measured across 1,428 configurations (n=3–60 × 119 seeds), and the generator carries a documented fallback that drops the plant rather than corrupt the term if every settlement already holds a linked refund, a state never observed — citing a payment outside this month's `payments.csv` — a real and expected shape (a refund for an earlier month's sale), and the case `REFUND_UNLINKED` exists for. Linked and orphan refunds are drawn onto **disjoint settlements**, not merely disjoint payments: truth publishes one `refunds_paise` per credit, so a settlement netting both would carry a term that is *partly* attributable, which this project's arithmetic has no name for. | 🟡 |
| 22b | **Rolling reserve (`--reserve`, Phase 6): 8% of settlements have 5–20% of their net held back, and the reserve is deliberately NOT modelled by the matcher.** The settlement declares its full net and the bank credit arrives short — the held amount appears in **no input file at all** (`settlements.csv`'s header is frozen by I9 and cannot gain a reserve column). Folding it into `net_paise` instead would make the mess non-existent, since the credit would then equal the declared net. The consequence is the design: a rule that fitted the shortfall would close **every** gap by construction, turning `UNEXPLAINED_RESIDUAL` rows into confident matches while every arithmetic gate stayed green. So the matcher *diagnoses* rather than resolves — a proportional second lookup at 2,600 bps, abstaining as `PARTIAL_SETTLEMENT_PENDING` and publishing no decomposition — and gate 13 asserts no resolved row ever carries a reserve term. The band's **lower bound is load-bearing**: the matcher separates "money was held back" from "the rates are wrong" by *size*, so the smallest reserve must clear the fee model's rounding divergence (a few paise) by orders of magnitude. A reserved credit is also the only row whose true settlement is absent from an exact-amount pool, so the generator nudges the held amount until the short credit matches **no** settlement's net in the run — otherwise a collision would yield one confident wrong candidate whose arithmetic closes perfectly, a silent wrong match rather than an honest abstention. | 🟡 |
| 22c | **What a reserved row's abstention does *not* prove.** Gate 13 shows the matcher declined to guess; it cannot show that declining was the only available answer, because with the held amount in no input file there is nothing to check against but truth's own record. That is a genuine limit of holding the reserve outside the net, and it is stated here rather than left for a reviewer to find. | 🔴 |
| 22d | **Unsettled payments (`--unsettled`, Phase 7): 2% of payments are captured and never pay out.** A real month always has some — a payment captured on the 31st settles in the next cycle, and one held for review may never settle at all — so a file where every payment reaches a settlement is the unrealistic case, not this one. The share is small on purpose: these rows are *supposed* to be a fringe the matcher leaves alone, and a large share would turn "most payments settle" into a false statement about the data rather than a fringe to be handled. Drawn after the refunds so it can see them and before `_make_nets_unique` so nets are placed against final memberships. **This share is coupled to `MAX_POOL` (#23b) and the coupling is tight enough to be a build constraint:** an orphan is declared by no settlement, so `tier1._tier2_pool`'s partition filter cannot remove it and it sits in every candidate pool whose window covers its capture date. At 2% the worst measured pool is 65 payments; at 5% it reaches 79 and at 15% it breaches the cap at 84. Moving this number means re-measuring that one, not interpolating. Truth publishes the orphan list at `orphans.unsettled_payment_ids`, and invariant I17 (`check_orphans`) asserts the count, the disjointness from every settlement's membership, and that no orphan reaches `settlement_items.csv`. | 🟢 |
| 22e | **Non-gateway bank rows (`--noise-rows`, Phase 7): 6% of the gateway credits a run produces, drawn uniformly over ₹500–₹50,000.** A real bank statement carries the whole business — vendor payments, salary credits, interest, an FD maturing — and the reconciler's first job is deciding which rows are even in scope. **The share is taken against the bank file rather than against `n`, and that is not cosmetic:** under `--batching` the two differ by roughly 1.6×, so a share of `n` would make noise nearly a third of a batched statement instead of a fringe. **The amount band overlaps the gateway bands deliberately.** A noise row separable by *size* would be separable without reading the narration at all, which would make the flag test a filter nobody needs; scope is a narration question, so the amounts are made useless for it. A noise row is a separate `NoiseRow` type rather than a `Credit` with empty provenance — `Credit.__post_init__` asserts non-empty `settlement_ids` and I8b indexes `payment_ids[0]`, so the alternative was weakening two checks that earn their keep — and the rows are merged into the credits' sort and numbered from **one** counter, so a `C####` says nothing about which kind of row it is. Invariant I18 (`check_noise`) carries 14 assertions over them, including that no noise narration offers a digit run matching a live settlement's UTR. **Writing it found two pre-existing defects of the same class**: I1's id-uniqueness check and I7's leak audit both ran over `story.credits` only, so both passed *vacuously* the moment a second kind of bank row existed. Neither was a suspension — a suspension names itself in `run_manifest.json`'s `checks_skipped`, while a population that quietly stopped covering the file announces nothing. | 🟢 |
| 22f | **The three noise strata are allocated by count, not drawn — 40% `plainly_foreign`, 30% `gateway_plausible`, 30% `look_alike` — and only the first is ignorable.** Allocation by largest remainder rather than a random draw is the load-bearing choice: `noise_recall`'s ceiling *is* the plainly-foreign share, so a drawn split would make that ceiling wobble seed to seed and gate 14 would have to assert a number loose enough to survive the wobble. Allocated, the realised counts are published in `run_manifest.json` under `noise_strata` and the gate asserts an **identity** instead of a threshold. The narration policy per stratum: `plainly_foreign` carries vendor, salary or interest text with **no gateway counterparty in any spelling and no settlement-hitting tail**; `gateway_plausible` carries a gateway counterparty with **no digit run at all** (the masked `XXXX` form, so `normalize.parse` reads no tail); `look_alike` carries a gateway counterparty **and** a 4-digit tail that hits no settlement's UTR. **The last two are deliberately not separable, and that is the point of the flag rather than a shortfall in it.** Both name the counterparty, so decision 4's rule (#25a) cannot set them aside; both fall through to the reserve diagnostic and score `NOISE_MISHANDLED`. Measured at n=1000 seed 42 with `--reserve --noise-rows`: **8 of 18 `gateway_plausible` and 9 of 18 `look_alike` rows are diagnosed as pending reserves** (`.plan/probe_phase7_band.py`), against 0 of 24 `plainly_foreign`. Relaxing the rule to close that gap is exactly what must not happen: under Phase 8's `--utr-patchy` a *genuine* gateway credit loses its tail and looks precisely like `gateway_plausible`, so a rule where "no readable tail" alone sufficed would convert this phase's recall into next phase's `WRONG_IGNORE` — money dropped from the books rather than merely left unexplained. **A recall of 100% here would therefore be a finding that the strata are too easy, never a win.** | 🟢 |
| 22g | **Foreign-currency payments (`--fx`, Phase 8): 8% of payments, one currency (USD), and the rate moves 10–200 bps between capture and settlement — sign drawn separately.** The mess is the *undeclared movement of a rate*, not a currency portfolio: a rate table is a rate the matcher can subtract, which closes the gap by construction. **Design (b) is locked** (`.plan/phase8.md` §1(c)): the payout is **correct** at the settlement-day rate and the **capture-rate gross in `payments.csv` is the stale number**, so the Tier 1 join survives and the residual is what cannot close. The plan's explainer called this "the reserve's twin", which is design (a) — credit disagreeing with a declared net — and the two are not interchangeable: (a) breaks the join and the reserve probe swallows the row, (b) breaks the residual. Measured over 12 cases at ±3/12/25%: design (a) gives `PARTIAL_SETTLEMENT_PENDING` throughout, design (b) `UNEXPLAINED_RESIDUAL`, and **no `NO_CANDIDATE` anywhere** on a single-member settlement. **8% is set by invariant I17, not by realism:** `--fx` and `--unsettled` draw independently, so an orphan can land on a foreign payment, and I17 asserts `orphan_currencies ⊆ settled_currencies` — which fails the moment *every* holder of a currency is an orphan. At 8% with one currency, n=60 draws ~5 foreign against ~1 orphan, so the failure needs all five orphaned at once; a wider `FX_CURRENCIES` re-opens that coincidence (~8% of `--fx --unsettled` runs at n=60 with four currencies) and needs re-measuring rather than interpolating. The 10 bps **floor** is the load-bearing bound: the largest rounding divergence this generator produces is 1–2 paise and the smallest gross band is 10,000p, so 10 bps on the smallest row is 10p — an order of magnitude clear of the rounding floor, below which truth would assert a mess the inputs cannot support. Verified 2026-08-29. | 🟢 |
| 22h | **`--fx` costs ~19% of coverage by voiding Tier 2's uniqueness inference, and that is the price of not guessing.** The headline Phase 8 measurement, and it is a *correction to this phase's own plan*, which asked gate 15 to assert coverage 1.0. When a foreign payment sits in the candidate pool its recorded gross is stale, so the subset search can no longer argue that the membership it found is the only one summing to the credit — and rows that would otherwise resolve abstain instead. Measured on the eleven-flag run, seeds 1/2/3 × n=200/1000: coverage **56.11%–71.20%**, with `FX_RATE_GAP` alone taking **137–148 rows at n=1000**. **Correctness stays 1.0 with 0 wrong matches on all six cells** — the search finds nothing rather than finding a coincidence, which is the whole distinction this project refuses to average. **The hole cannot be closed the way Phase 6's was.** Gate 13 found Tier 2 pricing each member four ways and never net of its refund; that was fixed with a *lookup*, because `refunds.csv` declares the refund and names its payment. The settlement-day rate is declared in **no input file** — that is the entire flag — so there is no fifth hypothesis to add and no table to consult. It can only be given an honest name. **Before step 5's fix, `--fx` on generator-drawn data produced 7 wrong matches** that the plan's hand-moved measurement could not see, which is why the inference is voided rather than merely widened. Consequences recorded elsewhere: `--fx` is a **fourth** `--dup-amounts` exclusion (#24e — a rate movement on one member of a planted pair separates the pair, and it fails on only half the seeds, which is worse than failing on all of them), and the matcher's `Decomposition` deliberately has **no `fx_paise` field** (#25b). Measured 2026-08-29. | 🟢 |
| 22i | **Missing UTRs (`--utr-patchy`, Phase 8): 15% of gateway credits lose the reference tail from their bank narration — and never from `settlements.csv`.** Decision 8, and the asymmetry is what makes truth's `resolvable: true` honest: the settlement's `utr` column survives, so the link genuinely exists in the inputs and a matcher that reads it can still resolve the row. 15% rather than a fringe because this flag's entire purpose is to attack `WRONG_IGNORE` and the attack must land on enough rows to be a test — ~9 credits at the n=60 default, **19 measured** at n=200 batched, 91–95 at n=1000. Partial for the reason every share here is partial: a run where *every* genuine credit lost its tail would not distinguish a matcher that reads the counterparty from one that had simply stopped ignoring anything. **A masked row is re-rendered into the noise vocabulary, not stripped, and that is measured rather than stylistic.** Deleting the 4 digits leaves `NEFT CR/RAZORPAYSOFT/`, `NEFT-RZRPAY-` and `NEFT/RAZORPAYSOFT/XXXX/SETTLEMENT` — and **3 of those 4 shapes are not producible by any noise row**, because `gateway_plausible`'s templates end in `XXXX`, `REF` or `SETTLEMENT` and never in a dangling separator. A masked genuine credit would then be identifiable *by shape alone*, so `WRONG_IGNORE == 0` would pass because the attack was visible rather than because decision 4's conjunction holds — I12's warning about absence-of-tail becoming "a tell unique to planted rows", arriving on a different flag. On #34 and #35: both feed the only figure in the submission that is neither measured
nor derived — "est. human time to clear". The honest position is that the shape of the
claim (exceptions are a small fraction of the batch) is robust, while the absolute
minutes are not. State them; do not defend them.

**The break-even the two assumptions imply, which the write-up must not walk past.** An
exception costs more per row than a routine tick-off, because the easy path already
failed. At 10 min per exception against a 2 min-per-row baseline, the tool saves time
only while exceptions stay **under 20% of bank rows** — below that the comparison
flatters, above it the honest reading is that a human would have been faster. The stub
fixture makes this visible on purpose: 60 exceptions score `10 h 00 min` against
`~2 h 00 min` by hand, which is the metric block correctly reporting that a matcher
which resolves nothing is worse than useless. Phase 13 should quote the ratio, not just
the minutes.

## Structural guarantees, not assumptions

Listed for completeness, because a judge is more likely to ask about these than
about the fee rates.

- `truth.json` is written to a **separate directory** from the CSVs, is read only
  through `hisaab/scoring/truth_io.py`, and `tools/check_isolation.py` fails the
  build if anything on the matching path can reach it.
- **Matched + exceptions = total, exactly.** No record is dropped. Enforced since
  Phase 2 in two places: `verdict_io.reconcile` refuses a verdict file that misses,
  duplicates or invents a bank row, naming the offending IDs, and `metrics.score`
  re-asserts `resolved + ignored + exceptions == total` before computing anything. A
  matcher that omits rows cannot score well by dropping the hard ones — it does not
  score at all.
- **The scorer reads the answer key; the validator does not.** `verdict_io.py` checks
  the matcher's output against plain values — the credit IDs to cover, the seed, the
  month — and imports nothing that could reach `truth.json`. It is the one scoring
  module deliberately absent from `check_isolation.py`'s allowlist.
- The matching engine is **deterministic by design**. No LLM sits on the match
  path; the model explains, triages, parses narration and answers Q&A, all
  downstream of every decision. Reproducibility and defensibility are the reason.
